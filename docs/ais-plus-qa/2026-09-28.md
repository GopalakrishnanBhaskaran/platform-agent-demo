### 1. Alexandra: How should I organize skills and context files so they work across Claude, Codex and other harnesses?
- Context: recent Claude changes (especially in Cowork) keep disrupting her setup. She wants skills, agent files and other context in one place she can use from any harness.
- Nate now works almost only in the **Claude Code and Codex desktop apps**, not VS Code. The apps handle multiple projects and browser use, and they show your files and folders. VS Code uses less CPU, so choose it if your machine is constrained.
- Claude now supports `AGENTS.md`, so one agent instructions file can serve both tools. Skills still live in separate folders: `.agents` for Codex and `.claude` for Claude.
- A **weekly routine** checks that the Claude and Codex skills folders match. You can run it more often or by hand.
- Everything (skills, projects) lives in a **private GitHub repo**. He pushes from his Windows desktop and pulls on his MacBook.
- He avoids in-app connectors and plugins because they lock you into one app. Keys go in a `.env` file, which he moves between devices with 1Password rather than email or chat tools.
- **Follow-up: How do you pull the skills onto your laptop?** His AIOS knows which private repo to push to. He just tells it to "pull in the latest commit" and starts working.
- Global settings and session logs stay on each device, so small memory preferences can differ between machines. He keeps nearly everything at project level. If you rely on global settings, sync those too.
- **Follow-up: What if I work in Cowork, which doesn't push or pull repos?** Nate has used Cowork for only about 30 minutes in total, so he had no recommendation.

### 3. Mateo: Which automation will restaurants actually pay for, and should first pilots be free or paid?
- Context: a read-only dashboard for independent restaurants that connects the till, staff planning and loyalty data. It sends a 7am WhatsApp brief on where margin is leaking. No paying clients yet.
- Restaurants pay where they already **leak money or feel the most pain**. You need a clear, known pain and a clear promise of how you fix it.
- The dashboard is probably a great add-on but a weak way in. Use it on the back end, not as the bait. It also builds trust in sales conversations.
- A dashboard alone doesn't improve the business, because the owner has to check it daily and act on it. Nate has had clients think they wasted money because the value wasn't clear to them.
- Mateo's long-term goal is a SaaS. The pains he sees are staff turnover, rising supply costs eating margin, and no-shows. He has worked in restaurants and has many contacts there, and Nate said to use them.
- **90-day plan:**
  - Offer one **free pilot** to a contact. It's a lower-pressure ask.
  - Learn their process and find their biggest pain and their first constraint.
  - Before building, agree on **one objective success metric** (e.g. tables booked per month, inventory left at month end) and record the baseline.
  - After launch, track the metric against the baseline to get a case study, a testimonial and referrals.
  - Repeat the same process with a few more restaurants, then templatize and productize it.

### 5. Yuri: What will still be surprisingly hard or unresolved in the next six months?
- The AI news cycle is brutal. Missing the latest model drop doesn't mean you're behind. What matters are the generational leaps that open new doors.
- Everyone is now building their own AIOS and skills, which creates **silos**. The next problem is getting teams to use them together.
- Data matters most: one real source of truth, with agents helping on data pipelines and project management.
- The core issue is **human inconsistency**. People forget to update things, and their mistakes get scaled across agents.
- Grokbot, Muse, Claude Code and Codex are personal agent builders. The next phase is **"multiplayer AI"**: humans and agents working together, with visibility into what the team builds, its runs and its data.
- The risk: put 10 employees with lots of agents into one system and things go wrong faster. A 100-person company becoming AI-native has to roll out safely. Nate's team is working on this and plans videos on it.

### 6. Vince: How do I land gigs and get taken seriously when I'm young?
- Being young is both an advantage and a disadvantage. Nate lost some deals over his age, and sometimes nothing helps.
- Many owners saw it as a plus: he was up to date on tech, moved fast and had used the tools longer than they had.
- Know your domain and recognize when something falls outside your expertise.
- **Start with free work.** For example, do 3 free gigs, over-deliver, and be professional and communicative. Results are proof no one can deny.

- Look in your **warm network**: friends' parents with businesses, family, co-workers, professors. Get real results and testimonials, then use them.
- AI is new, so everyone starts at roughly the same line. Years of experience aren't a big edge here.

### 8. Anon: How do I structure my AIOS into multiple agents that act as a marketing team?
- Context: a potential real estate agency client wants help with branding, social media, reels and website ideas. Claude Code would be the main runner, with client meetings every week or two.
- The goal is to drop in an idea (a video or a PDF) and have agents debate the hook and why it works.
- **Build a "council" around expertise you don't have.** Pick the best marketers or content packagers (e.g. Gary Vee, Alex Hormozi). Have research agents study their work, such as 500 Hormozi YouTube videos and his X posts.
- Turn each expert into a persona sub-agent with their quotes, principles and frameworks. Just asking AI to "build me a council" gives generic results.
- Build a **"marketing council" skill**. Give it an idea, and it brings in about 4 expert agents, runs rounds of debate and returns a consensus deliverable.
- Add **audience persona sub-agents** too: one that matches your avatar (e.g. a 30-year-old non-technical man in finance) and one that doesn't (e.g. a 50-year-old woman with a marketing background). See how each one reacts to the content.
- **Follow-up: Do I just ask Claude Code to build the agents?** Yes, and they'll live in your AIOS folders. Be clear on two things:
  - What you want: your vision and what it should look like.
  - How it knows it's done: what good looks like and how to check the output.
- Nate says 95% of his work is **"intent-driven building"**: state the intent, define what good looks like, and make the agent prove it's done.

### 9. Emran: Is there an orchestrator in the AIS+ classroom that routes requests across Claude Code, ChatGPT, Grokbot and Gemini?
- No, there's nothing like that in the classroom.
- To use your subscriptions rather than API billing, try existing tools first. T3 Code is a harness that wraps several subscriptions. Check open-source options before building your own.
- It's also fairly easy to vibe code with intent-driven building: explain the vision, define what good looks like and make it prove its work.
- **Follow-up: How do I optimize cost by routing each request to a model based on difficulty?** Use a cheap model such as JEV on the back end to decide the routing, and tune its prompt.
- Treat it as a hypothesis and prove it with **evals**. The more inputs and outputs in your dataset, the more you can trust the result.

### 10. Erik: My client wants an independent audit of the code my 11 agents build. How do I make sure it goes well?
- Context: a third-party firm will run the audit of the code and architecture on a long-term project. Erik's background is deployment and enterprise work, not hands-on coding.
- **While building:** give your agents software engineering principles from open-source GitHub repos written by experienced engineers. Matt Pocock has many good skills. Those principles haven't changed.
- **Before pulling in any repo:** ask Claude to read it without installing or running anything, and check it for malicious commands.
- **When the build is done:** use repos with security and architecture audit frameworks so you're not shipping spaghetti code. Run the audits yourself to find the gaps.
- List the worst failures your code could cause: data leaks, infinite loops, bugs. Send a fleet of agents to hunt for them with Codex, Claude Code and Grok Build, attacking from every angle and fixing in a loop.
- Some productized agents try to hack your code or leak data, then report where they succeeded.
- You then go into the third-party audit far more confident.

### 11. Kierra: How do I deliver my GitHub template to a new client? What should they invite me to, and do I set it up on their machine or remotely?
- Context: her first client meeting is the next day, for a social media workflow. The client has ChatGPT, no Git and messy folders. Kierra's framework includes code and automations that run locally.
- Nate first asked whether these are skills or automations in Trigger.dev or n8n. The answer depends on the stack.
- **Start with the experience they want:** something that runs in the background untouched, an always-on assistant they talk to, or something inside ChatGPT. Work backwards from that, because you can build the same solution 10 ways.
- **Accounts:** the client creates every account and API key, invites you as admin or engineer, and puts in their own card, so they pay for usage and subscriptions. You build in their environment.
- **Setup:** make it white-glove. Get on Zoom, have them share their screen and set things up together. Once you have the access and data, start building.
- **Follow-up: Could a plugin handle weekly post ideas plus ad hoc chat, and how would I see if it's working?** It could, but they'd have to send you screenshots of the results. If they want a ChatGPT-style experience and you need visibility, consider an app with an admin view.
- **Start with a proof of concept** rather than a custom app: an automation where inputs go in and a post comes out. Once they like the posts, build the UI.
- In the first meeting, ask what they want, how they'll use it, what it should look like and how they'll know it's good. Then design the milestones.

## Key takeaways
1. **Keep your AIOS portable:** one private GitHub repo, project-level skills and settings, a weekly sync between the Claude and Codex skill folders, and a `.env` file instead of in-app connectors. Save reusable sub-agents in `.claude/agents`.
2. **Sell a known pain, not a dashboard.** Run a free pilot, agree on one success metric up front, then turn the case study into referrals and a templated product.
3. **The next hurdle is "multiplayer AI":** one source of truth and team visibility, so human mistakes don't scale across agents.
4. **Free work in your warm network** builds the testimonials that outweigh doubts about age or experience.
5. **Ground agent teams in real expertise:** persona sub-agents built from experts' actual content, plus audience personas, run through a debate skill.
6. **Intent-driven building:** say what you want, define what good looks like and make the agent prove it. Back routing ideas with evals, and harden code with multi-harness audits before a third party looks.
7. **Client delivery:** the client owns accounts and billing, setup happens over screen share, and a simple proof of concept comes before any UI.
8. **Content:** write titles from your avatar's pain to get high-intent views, start editing with AI, and grow through consistency.
