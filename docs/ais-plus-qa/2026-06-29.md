### 3. Rafal: A voice-AI provider has done a discovery call and an MVP demo, and is refining it from my feedback. We haven't discussed pricing. Do I owe them anything?

### 4. Burhan: I built client products on localhost with Claude Code. What's the quick, professional way to hand them over? Should the client clone my repo?
- **Agree a definition of done per milestone,** so it's clear when you're paid.
- **Claude Code output is usually Python or TypeScript.** Decide where it runs:
  - Modal or Trigger.dev
  - A Claude routine on their account
  - Their GitHub plus Trigger.dev
- **Have a default way of working** ("we push your automations to Modal; you create the account and give us access") but stay flexible.
- **Hand over the GitHub repo plus documentation:** where it lives, why it's built that way, how to fix and improve it, and where the API keys are. Anyone should be able to maintain it. Cloning a repo is fine.
- **IP:** the client owns the code, workflows, agents, prompts and data. You keep your know-how and reusable components, granting them a non-exclusive right to use them, so you can reuse them across a vertical.
- **High-touch custom work** is how Nate's agency True Horizon reached $25K/month minimum retainers (3-month minimum). Each engagement refines your SOPs until you're "a well-oiled machine" ready to scale, rather than scaling a problem.
- Watch Nate's deployment videos ("3 ways to deploy Claude Code automations"). The n8n-era one still has relevant fundamentals.

### 6. Tom: We want to automate Meta campaigns end to end: copy, images, video, A/B tests. Where do we start?
- **Media buying is fairly rule-based** once trained, but keep a human on budget changes. You'd need a lot of trust to let anyone (or anything) raise a budget 200% in a day.
- Nate's team uses automation for reporting and recommendations, with a human clicking the final budget change. Creative strategy is the last thing to automate, because AI struggles to think outside the box.
- **Map the process:**
  - Write out the whole campaign process in order, from ideation to weeks of adjustments.
  - Break it into granular tasks, and branch at each decision point. Each bullet becomes a skill or automation.
- **Prioritise with the Eisenhower matrix and ICE scoring:**
  - **Impact:** time or money
  - **Confidence:** it will have that impact and can be built
  - **Ease:** how easy and quick it is to build
- Build from the top score down. Tip: ban the score 7 so people commit higher or lower.

### 7. Mike: Under strict GDPR, would you use hosted open models (GLM 5.2, MiniMax) for enterprises? Ollama's cheap plan can't take EU customer data.

### 10. Kierra: How do you tell know-how apart from IP?
- **Know-how is broad on purpose.** Say in the contract that any know-how you bring or develop during the engagement stays yours. That stops a client later claiming your method.
- **Name your processes,** like Bain's RAPID or Nate's four Cs and three Ms. It's easy to reference, signals experience, and lets the contract say methods used within your named framework stay yours.
- **IP is the tangible deliverables** (these Python files, these prompts). They become the client's on completion.
- **The grey zone is patterns you learn from seeing a client's data.**
- **A contract is only as good as the person on the other side.** If a big client seems combative, walk away: even a winning lawsuit costs time, stress and money.

### 11. Mike: Now that building is so accessible, when do I offer "I'll teach you" instead of "I'll build and deploy it"?
- **The line keeps moving** as development gets cheaper. You can't teach someone who isn't already partly convinced.
- **Work back from what the client wants:**
  - **Outcome buyers** arrive with "a PDF of 10 agents". Sell outcomes.
  - **Curious buyers** need "an AI strategy". Offer an audit.
  - **If an audit is too big an ask,** start with upskilling sessions. They uncover the best automations, then you build together or scope a project.
- **Build a roadmap or decision tree to use on calls:** "let's find where you are and how I can help you get where you want to go."
- **AI-native companies change from the top down.** In an IBM survey of about 2,000 CEOs, a large majority said every leader needs to be tech-fluent in their domain. Leaders learn AI first, then drip it down. Your job is to manage overwhelm.

### 12. Gary: What does an audit look like before scoping a project?
- **A paid discovery with a deliverable,** whether or not they hire you.
- **Nate's team delivered a 30–50 page PDF.** They agreed the length up front (one quick session or four one-hour sessions).
- **Find the constraints and bottlenecks,** then propose the top automations (say, three) that target them. Include the KPIs to watch and where they should move.
- **Next steps:** for example, meet with stakeholders to scope project 1, then price it.
- **Test:** the audit should be valuable even if they never work with you.

## Key takeaways
1. **Choose editing models by task:** GPT for following fixed templates, Opus for creative storytelling.
2. **A free MVP from a vendor isn't owed money.** Formalise scope, milestones, payment and done before going further.
3. **Hand over code with documentation and a clear default hosting method.** Clients own the deliverables; you keep know-how and reusable components.
4. **Break automation ambitions into a task tree and rank it** with ICE scores (no 7s). Keep humans on budget and creative decisions.
5. **For GDPR and cost,** start clients on open and local models now, and route tasks to the right model.
6. **Name your frameworks.** It protects know-how and signals experience.
7. **Offer a ladder:** upskilling, then audit, then build. Pick the rung from what the client wants, and manage overwhelm.
8. **A good audit stands alone:** constraints, the proposed automations, KPIs and next steps.
