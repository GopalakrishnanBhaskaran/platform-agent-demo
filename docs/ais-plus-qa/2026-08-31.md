# AIS+ Q&A: Aug 31, 2026

**Host:** Nate Herk · **Length:** 67 min · **Source:** Fireflies

## Q&A

### 1. Bob: I got locked out of my Claude account. How do I move my work into a new one?
- Context: Bob's appeal got no reply, so he signed up again with a different email. Most of his Claude Code work sits in a project folder on his desktop.
- Explain the situation to Claude Code. Ask it to search your machine for Claude's global settings, memory files and project folders.
- Claude Code writes everything locally, so most of your work should still be there. It may not recover everything.
- In the new account, open that same project folder in Claude Code. Your skills and memory files should carry over, and it should feel the same.
- The only real difference comes when you switch machines (say, desktop to laptop), because each machine has its own local configuration.

### 2. Merlijn: How can I run my business from my phone while I'm outdoors?
- It depends on the business. A solo SaaS is much easier to run remotely than a service business.
- From a phone you can come up with ideas, direct a team of agents, plan, and check outputs.
- The last 20%, building real processes and automations, is where Nate still sits at a desk. Otherwise untested work gets pushed into production.
- Context: Merlijn is building a mobile-friendly matchmaking platform, browsed a bit like Airbnb. It connects runners with brands, running clubs and causes. Profiles and introductions are all done by hand today.
- Automate onboarding and matchmaking first. Then keep attacking wherever you are the bottleneck: getting people in, onboarding, follow-ups.
- Expect to add a few employees or part-time contractors eventually.
- After that, the job is mostly maintenance, scaling and making the platform stickier.
- **Don't automate too much too fast**, or things break.

### 3. Ryan: Which classroom modules fit someone focused on the tools rather than consulting or marketing?
- Context: Ryan leads finance and ops at his firm. The firm has Claude (chat and Cowork) and ChatGPT Enterprise, but not Claude Code for everyone. He builds prototypes himself and wants to hand them to IT to make them production-ready.
- The course centers on Claude Code, but the principles work with any tool. Focus on three:
  - **Engagement design:** pick the right process, position it so the team buys in, and scope it properly.
  - **Evals:** run, say, 500 examples through a demo, score the results, tweak, and repeat. Build the simplest solution. Most "AI agents" end up as automations with no AI in them, and those are predictable, cheaper, less risky and faster.
  - **Governance:** what the automation touches, the risks, where the data goes and how it's protected. Explain it so everyone understands. IT may not know every industry regulation.
- Ryan added that running several Cowork tasks in parallel has roughly tripled his productivity.
- Nate's biggest client failures came from how engagements were set up, not from the tech:
  - **Scoping:** not knowing where the data lived, what it looked like, or whether there was an API.
  - **Pricing:** no pricing system, and milestones that were subjective and hard to prove.
  - **KPI:** align everyone on **one KPI** the project should move (e.g. from 5 to 12 in three months), with milestones that are objective and provable.

### 4. Eric: My ad-hook generator writes generic, AI-sounding copy. How do I get LLMs to a real creative bar?
- LLMs predict the next word, so they aren't built to think outside the box. Nate's president is a master copywriter, and the output still needs heavy editing even with his expertise fed in.
- To fix the AI sound, keep a team-wide **"AI phrase kill list"**. Anything you dislike goes on it, and the AI may not use those phrases or sentence structures.
- Build a style knowledge base. Every one of Nate's YouTube videos goes into a "Nate writing/speaking style" base.
- For creativity, have **several models debate** in a "war room". That can be Claude Code against Codex, a team of Claude agents, or Gemini, Grok, Claude and GPT together. Give them personas (copywriter, actor, professor) and have them poke holes in each other's ideas.
- Save a bank of the outlier outputs that do feel creative.
- **Follow-up: Does it need more human input or more input fields?**
  - Context: the client, a marketing agency, supplied 1,000+ indexed hooks, about 20 writing styles and golden examples. The tool also scrapes Reddit and Meta for competitor ads. The output is still junk.
  - You need an expert's knowledge pulled into the system, the way you'd train an intern.
  - Build an **AI judge of yourself**. Generate about 100 scripts, give feedback on each one, and build up the judging criteria from that.
  - The client had already rated about 50, mostly 1s and 2s. Nate agreed this is genuinely hard. One option is a harsh judge that only passes 5/5 scripts, so out of thousands maybe four get through.
  - Expect it to take 10x more work than you think.
- **Follow-up: Should the training material live in a RAG database or in markdown files?** Past a point, more examples just confuse the model. Use a simple system built on **negative prompts** (what not to do) rather than a thousand good examples.

### 5. John: What eval or debugging skills and agents can I use on a nearly finished CRM build?
- Context: John delivered a CRM for a client to test and has been running the code through Codex for periodic reviews.
- Look on X for engineering skills shared by the community. Nate recommends Matt Pocock's. Use skills built on real engineering principles, across a few different harnesses.
- Have a **fleet of agents try to break it**. For Nate's Calendly clone, agents opened the UI and clicked every button for 10+ hours. Feed the bugs they find back to agents that have the debugging skills.
- **Follow-up: How do you set those agents up?** A smart model (Nate uses Fable) acts as orchestrator and makes sure the edge cases get covered. The bug-finding agents can run on Sonnet or a cheaper model.
- Set expectations with the client: you'll do your best internal QA, but you can't catch every bug. Ask them to report feedback quickly.
- **Follow-up: How do I eval a qualitative feature?** In John's CRM, a user uploads a deal document and gets existing accounts ranked as tier 1/2/3 matches.
  - You need a **golden dataset** from the client: inputs paired with the correct outputs. The more the better.
  - Run every example and measure the match rate (say 75%). Change **one variable**, rerun (80%), and repeat. Change more than one and you won't know which change moved the score.
  - How it's built matters. In John's build, Claude Code processes data from Supabase. Nate noted that Claude Code typically isn't meant to be used programmatically, so this setup may not scale. It's also harder to test because an agent harness has more autonomy and gives less predictable results.
  - Nate would have the upload fire a webhook to the Claude API or the Agent SDK as a stateless call. Then Claude Code can make 100 calls, log the outputs in a Google Sheet, and compare them with the golden set.
- **Follow-up: Is there a special eval agent or LLM-as-judge for this?** No. An eval just means triggering the automation once per example. A sheet formula (does column H equal column J?) marks each one yes or no, but you still have to review the data yourself.

### 6. Carlos: How do I know my AIOS is set up correctly?
- There's no objective "correct" setup. Nate uses two mental tests:
  - **Intuition test:** could you find a document yourself by clicking through the folders? If it's intuitive for you, the agent will find it more easily too.
  - **Context failures:** poisoning (a wrong fact), confusion (missing or irrelevant facts), clash (two facts that contradict each other) and bloat (so much that the agent can't find what matters). When you hit one, it's time to restructure.
- Nate's whole AIOS CLAUDE.md is **routing rules**. It works like a table of contents: business context is here, YouTube transcripts are here, projects are here.
- If it can't find something, or takes five minutes to find a file you know exists, ask it to walk you back through what it did. Then add a routing rule or restructure.
- Connect the tools you use most (e.g. Slack). Set up a daily automation that pulls Fireflies transcripts into the AIOS, and a quarterly one that removes the old ones.
- Set up these regular jobs so the AIOS cleans and updates itself. Nate has been building his for about six months and is now working on wikis and cleanup processes.
- **Follow-up: Is your two-hour AIOS course still relevant?** Yes. Much of it is about mindset and isn't tied to any one person's setup.

### 7. Mohammed: Should I sell my insurance-claims agent bespoke to one insurer or build it as a product for many?
- Context: the agent reads policy wordings, claim files and adjuster reports. It then drafts a coverage position, citing the policy clause by clause, for a human to sign off.
- Ask how long it would take to adapt for the next client: 1 hour, 10 hours, or a week?
  - If it's a prompt change or a few tweaks, and the process is similar across the industry, turn it into a template.
  - If setup takes long, it needs white-glove service (pulling knowledge out of the client, writing prompts). That makes a great foot-in-the-door offer, but it's hard to scale.
- Other choices to make:
  - Be a high-touch consultant, or be the best at one process.
  - Host it as a subscription, or sell it as a one-time product.
- Bespoke, high-touch work needs clients with bigger budgets. A do-it-yourself SaaS suits smaller businesses.
- **Follow-up: So it mostly comes down to my target audience?** Partly. The bigger question is what it takes for customers to **get results**, and you need hard numbers on that. If the product alone gets results, scale it as a product. If results need consulting and setup, sell that.

### 8. Cristian: What tools can automate video creation for my cybersecurity academy, and how do I grow the community?
- Have Claude Code or Codex orchestrate:
  - Asset generation with Higgsfield or Key AI.
  - An **ElevenLabs professional voice clone**, generated in **30–45 second clips**. Longer audio degrades after about a minute.
  - HeyGen Avatar 5 to make video of you from that audio.
  - Hyperframes for editing, motion graphics and animations.
- **Verification is the key step.** After a video is generated, have agents screenshot the frames and check that nothing is out of bounds.
- It's slow. Turning a 15-minute raw recording into a 10-minute edited video took about 1.5 days and about 15 versions.
- **Growing the community:**
  - Lead with as much free value as you can, possibly as a freemium model.
  - Capture emails to build a list you can run promotions to.
  - Grandfather early members in and build a culture.
- **Follow-up: n8n content leaves AIS+ by Sept 30. Should we focus only on Claude Code?** Yes. Agentic harnesses like Claude Code and Codex build much faster, and you own what you build. Using both tools together was confusing people.
  - There's no lock-in. Nate uses Grok, Hermes, Claude Code and Codex every day on the same AIOS. If all four disappeared, he'd switch to DeepSeek or OpenCode. You're betting on yourself and your AIOS, not on one tool.

### 10. Mike: What retainer model works after implementing an AIOS for a business?
- While you build the AIOS, you learn the business, spot its constraints, build trust and prove your expertise.
- Service ladder:
  - Start with education and consulting hours, or a project setup fee.
  - Pitch an **audit of the whole business**, not just one person's setup, to find the best automation opportunities.
  - Move to a formal scope of work. Charge a one-time project fee, or pitch a retainer if they're bought in and you've built trust.
- An AIOS is **never finished**. Clients keep running into issues, asking how to scale and connecting new tools, so you become "the AI person" for the business.

### 11. Ashmit: Should I go vertical (freight insurance brokers) or sell a lead-gen OS to AI startups, and when should I start charging?

### 12. Anon: As a newbie who feels behind, how do I prioritize what to learn?
- **Pick one project that helps you.** You are your own best proof.
- Build things you'd use every day or week, such as an AI news tracker or a weekly competitor-research automation. Things you won't use teach you little.
- Then tackle your next constraint. If you have no leads, build lead-gen and follow-up automations. After that, proposal writing and meeting analysis.
- "Build your own plane while you fly it." When you pitch clients, you can show what these tools did for your own business.

### 13. Yuri: Are we still early, or is AI moving so fast that it's already too late for consultants?
- "It's hard to see the label when you're inside the jar." Most people who don't watch AI YouTube every day are far behind. Enterprises move slowly, and small businesses lack the education and resources.
- It's not a question of being too late but of **trust**. Anyone can build an impressive portfolio in a few hours now, so stand out with proof, credentials and content.
- It won't be too late for anyone who keeps building proof and trust, and many new roles will open up.

## Key takeaways
1. **Start with one project that helps you.** Your own results are your best proof. Then work through your constraints one at a time.
2. **Most failed projects fail on the engagement, not the tech.** Scope carefully, use objective milestones, and agree on one KPI the project should move.
3. **An eval is a golden dataset plus repeated runs.** Change one variable at a time. Prefer simple, stateless automations (often with no AI) that are easy to test.
4. **LLMs aren't creative by default.** A phrase kill list, multi-model "war room" debates, a harsh judge and negative prompts help.
5. **Keep the AIOS easy to navigate and clean.** Make CLAUDE.md a set of routing rules, watch for poisoning, confusion, clash and bloat, and automate syncs and cleanups.
6. **Go bespoke before you build a product.** Productize only when adapting it for a new client is quick and the product alone gets results.
7. **An AIOS build leads to a retainer.** Go from setup to a business-wide audit, then a scope of work, then ongoing support, because an AIOS is never finished.
8. **Stay tool-agnostic.** n8n content is leaving AIS+. Bet on agentic harnesses and your own AIOS rather than one vendor, and win clients with trust and proof.
