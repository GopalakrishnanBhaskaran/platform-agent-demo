# AIS+ Q&A: Jun 1, 2026

**Host:** Nate Herk · **Length:** 67 min · **Source:** Fireflies

## Q&A

### 1. Joe: Obsidian with Claude, or the Google Workspace CLI, for a team wiki and second brain? How should I structure and host it for a client team of 5–7?
- **Context:** Joe's first attempt (Claude filling a Google Drive through an ingestor) got lost finding folders. The client wants department SOPs, frameworks, course material and call transcripts in one searchable place.
- **Open-loop vs closed-loop organisation** (from a Garry Tan Stanford talk):
  - In an open-loop company, decisions live in Slack threads and email, "on vibes".
  - In an AI-native, closed-loop company, everyone's agents can read everyone's shared processes and decisions.
  - Local-only setups break this.
- **Nate's preference:** a cloud data store (Google Workspace or Notion) that everyone can read, and only certain people can write to (or only to certain folders).
  - The alternative is separate local stores per client with an endpoint (say, a chatbot) that queries them.
  - GitHub works, but many people pushing gets messy.
- **What decides success is behaviour, not infrastructure:** whether people sync their data, use it, and default to the new way.
- **Ask what the end user actually needs.** An Obsidian graph view, or just confidence that agents pull from real-time, accurate data? Nate rarely opens Obsidian himself.
- **Automate the syncing.** Nate's team pipes every Fireflies transcript into a shared "data lake", segmented by access (policies and team comms for everyone, some content leadership-only).
  - His agency used to joke it was "a data infrastructure agency" because so much client work was migrating and syncing data.
  - That work is mostly deterministic scripts: cheap, low-maintenance quick wins.
- **Change is hard.** One narrative now: an AI-native "twin" of one business unit is easier than transforming the whole org. Joe is starting with "wiki first".
- **Joe's client already lives in Google,** so Drive plus the Google Workspace CLI is a safe bet. Everyone working from their own local store is the thing to avoid.

### 2. Wasim: I'm bringing AIOSes to independent pharmacies but I'm not very technical. Do I install the AIOS first, or the specific tool they asked for (an NDC reimbursement optimiser)? How do I handle HIPAA?
- **The pharmacy background is an edge.** He grew up in independent pharmacies, and the first prospect (a family member) has five.
- **Lead with the AIOS-and-education route.** It builds trust, and while you connect their data and learn their processes you are effectively doing the audit. Be deliberate about which pain points you dig into.
- If they push back ("I don't have time to learn this"), pivot to the classic audit then project.
- **Be careful with connections.** Don't connect MCP servers "willy nilly". Assume that if the AIOS can do something, it eventually will. For each connection, ask: why do we need it, and what's the worst case?
- **HIPAA:** before choosing hooks or manual CSV exports, find out whether patient data can be sent to a closed-source model at all without putting the practice at risk.

### 3. Gary: I built my AIOS. Now I'm adding the executive assistant. Do I drop its folders into the OS root, or build the EA separately?
- For Nate, the EA *became* the OS: the same folders, files, skills and knowledge. Figure out what the EA has that your setup lacks (usually autonomy and automation) and add that.
- **To merge safely:**
  1. Commit and push the current OS to GitHub so you can roll back.
  2. Clone a copy.
  3. Give Claude the EA prompt and ask it to add the new folders while keeping all `CLAUDE.md` routing and context files consistent.
- **Two tests for structure:**
  - You understand what lives where when you look at the file tree.
  - Claude reads the right files when prompted. If you see it searching folder after folder ("grep, grep, grep"), your routing needs work, and it's wasting input tokens.
- Nate runs a pass every so often: "Do you know where everything lives? Should we consolidate anything or add lines to `CLAUDE.md`?"
- **Chat or Code?** The VS Code extension *is* Claude Code. Nate always uses the CLI now, mainly for the status line and slash commands.

### 4. Rod: An $11M company wants me as a fractional Chief AI Officer. How do I structure a 90-day sprint proposal?
- **Lead with an audit.** Take emotion out and find the numbers that need to move.
- **Two questions:**
  1. "If 10× your customers showed up tomorrow, what would break first?" It forces them to walk through the business in order, and surfaces the true constraint.
  2. "What would it take to get you 10× more customers tomorrow?"
- **Give the project one KPI tied to the bottom line.** Example: support response time from 3 hours to 5 minutes, and what that's worth.
  - Ad-agency results are obvious ("$10K in ads, this many customers"). Operational automation isn't, so it's your job to make the value visible.
  - Decision-makers often don't feel background systems working.
- **One project can fill the 90 days.** Phase it from proof of concept to deployment, watch real-world metrics for weeks, and iterate ("it's moved this much, we still want it here").
- **Cadence:** Nate's team ran biweekly meetings, stayed active in shared ClickUp channels, and sent Loom progress updates. Get the actual process owners, and sometimes engineering, on calls, not just the C-suite who bought.

### 5. Dan: What's the most efficient way to get a process out of an operator's head? I do 2–3 discovery calls plus their Looms.
- **Two phases:** first make sure the current process works (automating a broken one scales the breakage), then automate.
- **On the call,** share a screen (Excalidraw or a Google Doc). Have them walk through it step by step while you write numbered bullets.
- Every 2–3 bullets, check: right order? Missing nuance? What are the dependencies, and what happens if X instead of Y?
- **Be "annoying":** people skip steps they know by heart. Ask about worst cases and input types. Get other stakeholders on if the process touches them.
- Testing will surface more edge cases. You can't catch them all up front.
- Break the job into responsibilities and subtasks. One "process" is often four chained together, so start with the first.

### 6. Emanuel: I have a website, case studies and LinkedIn posts, and I cold call, but I'm not landing clients. What would you recommend?
- **His problem:** he rarely reaches managers or owners. Gatekeepers blow him off, and he's unsure of his niche.
- **Watch Nate's interview with Savon** on cold email and list building. List building is one of the hardest parts.
- **The market may need to mature.** Upwork already has buyers who've posted the job and are bought in, so it's just the right case study and proposal.

### 7. John: I'm building a knowledge base from recordings, but much of the value is on screen (slides, screen shares). How do I extract the visuals?
- **A two-stage pipeline:**
  1. Use a vision model to describe the slides, screenshots or video segments.
  2. Pair those descriptions with the transcript in the knowledge base, so retrieval returns both the text and the source.
- **Check existing tools first** (SaaS or open-source repos). If none fit, orchestrate it yourself:
  - Build a skill that finds moments worth capturing, such as when the speaker refers to a diagram or a prompt that isn't in the transcript.
  - Grab those frames, then run them through Claude or a Google vision model. Even a blurry screenshot usually works.

### 10. Angel: A guest said "we took the logic of a business and converted it into a system". Do you agree, and how would you explain it to prospects?
- **Mostly.** Nate's sequence with every business was the same:
  1. Extract the know-how.
  2. Sync the data and get it right.
  3. Ask what the problems are, and solve them with as little AI as possible.
- He'd phrase it "converted into an AI-*ready* system". If you can solve a task without AI, do.
- Like the label "AI consultant", the flashy framing is what businesses want to hear now, but won't be needed later.

### 12. Andrew: Is there a niche that's quietly working but most of the community ignores?

- A niche can be a process or a vertical, not just an industry ("the best sales agents for anyone").

### 14. Joe (follow-up): How did your team build its Drive-based second brain? Should ingest use skills? Cowork or Code?
- **They didn't set out to build a "second brain".** The company Drive already had folders (business ops, AIS, AIS+, events).
- **What made it agent-ready:** a table of contents that tells the agent where things live. Without one, it searches everything from the start every time.
- They're tackling it now because everyone had built their own executive assistant and agents in their own way.
- **Even simple repeated prompts deserve a skill.** Nate's session-handoff skill is just "summarise what we did, what's open, the next decisions and the files", which he got tired of typing.
- **Ingest:** the agent should infer where content goes from minimal context, but people must check that uploads land where they'd put them by hand. Improve the ingest logic over time.
- **Tool:** Nate does everything in Claude Code. It's the most powerful option.

## Key takeaways
1. **A team second brain needs a shared cloud store** that everyone can read and only some can write to, plus a table of contents for agents. Local-only stores never stay real-time.
2. **Adoption is the hard part.** Automate syncing (for example, transcripts into a data lake), and segment access by role.
3. **Routing is good when Claude finds files in one shot.** Repeated grepping means `CLAUDE.md` routing needs work. Commit before you restructure, so you can roll back.
4. **Connect tools deliberately.** Assume an AIOS will use any access it has. Check compliance (HIPAA, PII) before sending data to a closed model.
5. **Find the true constraint** by asking "what breaks first at 10× customers?", then tie the project to one bottom-line KPI and make its value visible.
6. **Extract processes step by step on a shared screen.** Ask about dependencies and worst cases, and split chained processes.
7. **Make the business AI-ready first:** know-how and data, then the least AI that solves each problem.
8. **Client acquisition:** warm outreach, inbound and Upwork beat cold email and cold calls.
