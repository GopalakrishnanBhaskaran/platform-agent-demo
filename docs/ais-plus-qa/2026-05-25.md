## Q&A

### 1. Tanya: What goes into contracts, SLAs and ongoing maintenance? And what do I say when a client asks "why not just buy X and have you implement it"?
- **Context:** Tanya does the strategic diagnosis; partners implement and maintain. She'll be the hands-on contact at first, to learn where problems appear.
- **Build a branded framework.** Development keeps getting cheaper; expertise doesn't. Protect your value with a named way of working, as McKinsey and Bain do, even if the work underneath is similar.
- **Support terms:** Nate's early packages had a guaranteed response time (12 or 24 hours by tier). That turns a subjective promise into an objective one you can prove with email and Slack timestamps.
- **IP was the biggest contract question:** clients own their data and systems; you protect your know-how and any reusable components or internal frameworks you bring.
- **Write contracts assuming the other party will try to take advantage.** You draft them while everyone is excited, but they exist for when things go wrong.
- **Nate's landmines were scope, not service:**
  - Milestone definitions were subjective ("this feature is there"), so clients disputed whether a milestone was met before the next payment. Make definitions of done objective.
  - In one March they took on 11 clients, too many for the team.
  - They gave some clients personal phone numbers, with no boundaries, and got calls in the middle of the night. Be there for clients, but set boundaries.
- Nobody has a "right" answer yet. Even Chief AI Officer roles are invented on the spot.
- **Build vs buy:**
  - Buying a $20/month SaaS first is often right: automating a broken or missing process just scales the problem.
  - Using a bought tool for a few months effectively writes your requirements document for a later custom build.
  - In 15–20 years most businesses will run mostly custom software, often on open models. Recommending "buy" can mean less revenue now, but it wins trust and more business later.

### 2. Cheryl: A bank wants more than a vendor's basic banking agents, on its own servers. Do I build separately, and how do I deploy on-prem?
- **Taking on unfamiliar work:**
  - If you have steady demand, don't take on work you're not comfortable with.
  - If you need experience, take it, but be transparent about what you don't know, and position yourself as the one who figures it out safely.
- **Talk to their IT or on-prem expert,** not just the CEO. The CEO is probably feeling board and competitor pressure and won't know the on-prem or open-source trade-offs.
- **Unpack the pressure.** About 4 in 5 owners Nate meets want "an agent" because they feel they need an AI strategy, but the bottleneck that matters is often solvable without AI.
- **Pick one KPI as the North Star** ("move X from 10% to 15%"). It takes emotion out, sets your success criteria, and tells the client why they're investing.
- **Is Cloudflare secure enough?** It's a reputable company, but it's case by case. Nate wouldn't take on regulated, unfamiliar work without a subject-matter expert on the team. He has "chewed glass" and refunded projects to protect his reputation.
- Cheryl's framing for the client is a good one: an agent is like a new employee, and you don't give it the keys to the kingdom.

### 3. Brandon: Can time saved be a KPI? How would I document it?
- It can be, but it's subjective. Time saved usually unlocks something else. Find what it unlocks and measure that, while still reporting hours saved per employee per week.

### 4. Tola: Should I still build the AIOS first, or build 3–5 projects and then look for clients?
- Nate's video on starting with education and collaboration was aimed at imposter syndrome: freezing on discovery and sales calls even with a portfolio.
- If that's you, a low-pressure collaborative start is better. If it isn't, you don't need that rung. It's a lever you can pull when needed.

### 5. Andres: Same as Tanya. Legal liability, SLAs, SOWs, insurance. Who should I talk to?
- **A contract is only as good as the person on the other end.** Even a bulletproof one won't stop someone determined to sue, and fighting it costs time and fees. Trust your gut about who you work with.
- Talk to a real lawyer. Nate's team found one on retainer in their state (they used Perplexity to research where to look). He uses AI to draft and review contracts and spot risks, but never blindly.
- **Add an inactivity clause:** if the client doesn't respond for 30 days, you can close the engagement. That once happened to Nate's team, and the clause is now in every contract.
- **Contracts evolve:**
  - Early on you collaborate and accept redlines.
  - With enough engagements behind you, split them into a stable master services agreement ("how we work") and a scope of work that changes per project.
  - At that point pushback is mostly clarification questions, usually about IP.

### 6. Angelo: I use Trigger.dev and only have the Claude API. How do I run my local skills in the cloud? Is Managed Agents the answer?
- A skill assumes an agent loop. Wireframe each one first:
  - If it's a fixed order of steps, it's really a workflow. It may just be a Python script calling things in order.
  - If it's "here are six possible steps, use them as needed", it needs an agent.
- **For agent-style skills, use the Claude Agent SDK** (what Claude Code is built on). It gives you sessions and the agent harness, and can run skills from Trigger.dev.
- For community skills, open the markdown file and see what it does, or ask Claude to explain it.
- **Managed Agents:** Nate hasn't used it much. When he tried it, it looked aimed at people who've never used Claude Code. If you're fluent in Claude Code, you can do the same yourself. It has been updated since.

### 7. Gregory: I'm a non-technical solo marketing consultant. Can I build a creative-director agent with copywriter and designer sub-agents? Where do I start?
- **It's valuable but a tall task.** Gregory has the advantage of 20 years of subject-matter expertise.
- **Think of a role as a tree.** The role is the trunk, its job-description bullets are the branches, and each branch has many leaves (specific subtasks). You don't automate "a creative director"; you automate 80 leaves, one a week, and piece them together.
- **Nate's example:** "automate a YouTube video" would go badly. "Pull my comments daily and give me the pain points and ideas" works beautifully, then you automate the next leaf.
- **Start with the SOP:** write out, in chronological order, how you'd do the work by hand. That becomes the first branch (a skill), and the tool calls inside it are the leaves.
- **Which tool?** Claude Code. Most of Nate's content and the community course are built on it. Codex and Hermes do similar things.
- **Being non-technical is fine,** but understand what the AI built and why, or you'll end up with "dark code". Ask about edge cases and it will add protections.

### 8. Mattia: For an AIOS demo, which data or use case makes the strongest first MVP?
- **Start from pain points.** For Nate it was communication: what he discusses with his team, clients and meetings. His executive assistant began with comms and task management, then grew into his AIOS.
- **Seven categories to work through** (from his AIOS video), by where the owner's pain is: revenue, customers, calendar, comms, tasks, meetings, knowledge.

### 9. Shawn: An enterprise legal director wants internal Copilot to capture his review and coaching style for junior staff overseas. Do I scope it as a workflow audit, an SOP pack or a 30-day pilot? How do I price it and limit risk?
- Copilot would be a poor place to build this.
- **Two phases:**
  1. **Extract his knowledge into a text SOP, with examples, before any AI.** The hard part is getting the IP out of his head.
  2. **Build an interface (such as a chatbot) only if the SOP works.** Success criterion: juniors read it and measurably improve.
- In a regulated Fortune 500, some things can't be built yet. Doing the extraction now means you're ready when they get the green light.

### 10. Angel: What do you do first when you start a new workspace?
- **Build it as you go:**
  - Have Claude help write `CLAUDE.md` from the goal and key resources.
  - Add a settings file for what it may and may not do.
  - Start building skills.
  - When brainstorming, tell it what you're building, where it will evolve, and the starting folders.
- **Restructure continuously, using Claude:**
  - His `CLAUDE.md` files change almost daily, and project folder architecture weekly.
  - In his AIOS, the big categories (projects, other areas, docs) stay fairly static, but the AIOS `CLAUDE.md` changes daily.
- Don't worry about building it wrong. Assume it will evolve with you.

### 11. AW Solutions: What's your YouTube production process? I can't record a 2-minute scripted video without mistakes.
- **Tutorials and case studies:** no word-for-word teleprompter. Nate knows the material, plans about five key scenes, keeps bullets and key stats on a side monitor, and talks. He edits his own videos and often moves sections around.
- **VSLs and ads:** he always uses a word-for-word teleprompter.
- **Cheaper options:** the iPhone's built-in teleprompter, a solid teleprompter for about $200, or (less preferred) AI eye-contact correction.

### 13. Cheryl: Do hold-harmless agreements hold up for AI agents and SaaS?
- **Roll out in phases.** Nothing goes live without the client's explicit approval, and that approval shifts responsibility for the system's outputs to them.
- **Spell out client requirements:** feedback, reviews and examples (if you need 100 good email examples, they provide them).
- **The mindset:** assume an agent will eventually use any data it can read and any tool it can execute, even if not instructed. Framing it that way makes everyone more careful.

## Key takeaways
1. **Make every contract term objective:** response times, milestones and definitions of done. Add an inactivity clause, set boundaries, and protect your know-how and reusable IP.
2. **Buy before you build** when the process isn't proven. A bought tool writes your requirements for the custom version.
3. **Anchor each project on one KPI** to take emotion out of the decision. Time saved matters most for what it unlocks.
4. **Don't take on unfamiliar, regulated work** without a subject-matter expert. Talk to the client's IT people, not just the owner.
5. **Turn skills into code where you can:** a fixed sequence is a script, and agent-style skills run in the cloud through the Agent SDK.
6. **Automate a role leaf by leaf,** starting from your own manual SOP.
7. **Start an AIOS where the pain is,** usually comms and tasks. Revenue, customers, calendar, comms, tasks, meetings and knowledge are the categories.
8. **Extract knowledge into an SOP before adding AI.** If people can't use the document, an agent won't help.
9. **Workspaces evolve:** `CLAUDE.md` changes daily, folders weekly, and the top-level categories rarely.
10. **Assume an agent will use every tool and every piece of data it can reach.** Phase rollouts and get client sign-off.
