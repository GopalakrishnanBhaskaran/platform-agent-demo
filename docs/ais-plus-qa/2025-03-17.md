# AIS+ Guest Speaker: Dan Martell, Mar 17, 2025

**Host:** Nate Herk · **Guest:** Dan Martell · **Length:** 59 min · **Source:** Fathom

A guest session rather than a weekly Q&A. Dan Martell (serial founder, angel investor, *Buy Back Your Time*, SaaS Academy, Martell Ventures) answered Nate's questions, presented a framework, then took one question per member.

## Opening talk

### Nate: What will AI and agents do to SaaS?
- **The interface is moving** from CRUD screens to voice and chat.
- **Subscriptions give way to "results as a service":** outcome- or usage-based pricing. Many of Dan's AI investments have already moved their monetisation this way.
- **Opportunity:** build an AI front end on top of an existing tool (a CRM, workflow software) to own the customer experience, then expand.

### Nate: My agency (True Horizon) is bottlenecked on delivery of custom work. What should we think about?
- **Three growth engines:** awareness (marketing), conversion (sales) and delivery (fulfilment). Use the **theory of constraints** to find where the workflow jams.
- **Productise services into modules.** Even if you sell 15 configurations, each module becomes a building block you can repeat, document, train on and hire specialists for. Solo founders underestimate how many hats they wear.
- **Scale in pods:**
  - A pod of value is, for example, a UX designer, two engineers, QA and a researcher.
  - People can sit on more than one pod (a designer at 50% on two, one data scientist across three).
  - Add pods to scale horizontally. One person owns the roadmap and routes work to pods.
- Dan built a $5M agency in his early 20s without knowing this. All his companies now run on pods.

### The AI adoption ladder
Five levels, each with a function and a typical frequency of use. Have clients rate where they are, then sell them the next rung. "When you draw a picture, you draw them in."

| Level | Function | Frequency | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Play | Chat | Monthly | Most people still under-use chat. Teaching a company to use the tools it already has is an opportunity in itself. |
| 2. Process | SOPs | Weekly | Record yourself doing the work (screen share, talking out loud), feed the video to AI and have it write the SOP. Someone else maintains it. Tools: Trainual, NotebookLM. The lowest-hanging fruit. |
| 3. Product | AI-powered tools | Daily | Every team must use an AI tool. Dan's finance team found an AI fraud-detection and reporting tool. His media team was spending two hours per video on B-roll until they found a tool. You can get paid to be "an advanced search tool" for the right tools. |
| 4. Protocol | APIs | Hourly | Custom solutions: you conduct APIs rather than train models. Examples: a lead-scoring algorithm written with ChatGPT and installed as a CRM webhook, and pulling the shipping address from scanned purchase orders by giving the image to AI. |
| 5. Pioneer | Custom models | Real time | Building models and generative video. Dan spends time on Hugging Face Spaces. He records everything so a future AI version of him ("generative wisdom") can take 1,500 calls at once. |

## Q&A

### 1. Juan: With my corporate background, I tend to over-engineer. How do I keep things simple and get first traction?
- **Early tech-stack investment is mostly wasted.** Things change so fast you'll rebuild anyway, and no customer brags about your infrastructure.
- **Ask how it improves the customer experience.** Customers ask "when can I have it, and is it buggy?"
- **Ship real product fast, then iterate.** Fast and not buggy gets referrals; anything else adds no perceived value.

### 2. Pascal: A thank-you
- His 14-year-old son became proactive after they watched Dan's "what I'd tell my 20-year-old self" video together.

### 3. Ken: What does "SaaS is dead" really mean? Will only a few big companies deliver solutions?
- **Dan started Martell Ventures** (an AI venture studio) six months earlier because even after 27 years in software he didn't know where this was going, and wanted to be close to it.
- **Perceived value follows frequency of use.** People value Slack and Zoom because they use them daily.
- **Customers prefer to pay only for usage.** His investment FlexPay reduces card declines; customers wanted to drop the setup and monthly fees and pay only the metered rate.
- **The future is agents as services** that do one thing best and plug into other tools. Browser automation now works through a tool's front end even when there's no API.
- **Interfaces become a phone number.** Boardy, a business-networking agent, has no app: you call it and it books meetings.
- **SaaS is "dead" because of the subscription,** not because people stop wanting innovation.
- **Differentiate with a personal brand.** Trust has moved from networks to individuals, and media is leverage.

### 4. James: My wife (a fractional CFO) and I built an AI tool that turns accounts into plain-language financial reports. It's a working local prototype tested with clients on Xero and QuickBooks. When do we seek help or investment?
- **The best financing is customer financing.** Sell before you build (wireframes are enough). Consultancies sell custom solutions that don't exist yet all the time.
- **Investors are risk-averse.** "Has anyone paid you for it?" Separate idea validation from product-market fit.
- **The "Founder 50" approach:** get about 50 customers to pay $500–1,000 each, which funds the MVP, then raise.
- **Decide who the customer is:** accounting firms or entrepreneurs. Dan's own agency product ended up selling to agencies.
- **With consumption pricing,** utilisation becomes your proxy for product-market fit.

### 5. Aron: Can AI replace the first hire, the assistant?
- **You can delay that hire.** Run the "buyback loop" from his book (audit, transfer, fill) and automate up to about 90% of tasks like email processing.
- **Dan's chief of staff works with an AI consultant** who automates her work. He expects a dedicated "SysOps" role in companies, like RevOps.
- **An assistant is a partner,** not an automation. They hold you accountable, and "you'll do more for other people than you'll ever do for yourself". He'd sell his car collection before giving up his assistant.

### 6. Mazey: I have a website, an AI agent and a booking funnel, and I'm on every platform. How do I get clients and scale?
- **Use "other people's platforms":** podcasts and stages, virtual or in person.
- **First, make your own profiles worth finding.** Hosts will look you up, and private accounts with no content won't get booked. Be "easy to find, hard to get hold of."
- **Share everything you do for free.** Dan's agency model is free online, and it brings people into his world.
- **"You either pay or publish."** Publishing builds long-term brand equity. "It's not who you know, it's who knows you."

## Key takeaways
1. **SaaS is moving to "results as a service":** outcome- and usage-based pricing, with voice and chat as the interface.
2. **Fix delivery bottlenecks with the theory of constraints.** Productise services into reusable modules, and scale with pods.
3. **Use the AI adoption ladder** (play, process, product, protocol, pioneer) to diagnose clients and sell the next step.
4. **Don't over-engineer.** Ship working, bug-free product fast, and iterate on customer value.
5. **Fund products with customers first** ("Founder 50"), then raise.
6. **AI can delay your first hire,** but a great assistant is a partner, not an automation.
7. **Build a personal brand:** publish freely, then appear on other people's platforms.
