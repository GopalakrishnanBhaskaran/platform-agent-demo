# AIS+ Q&A: Sep 21, 2026

**Host:** Nate Herk · **Length:** 50 min · **Source:** Fireflies

## Q&A

### 1. Mike: How do I bring Claude Code and Codex into Azure-based corporate teams? Is the AIOS form still open?
- **AIOS form:** Still open. Nate had unpinned it, so he re-shared the link in chat. Members can send in AIOS builds they've created or delivered to clients.
- Context: Mike's corporate clients run their database and deployments in Azure. They want to use Claude and Codex, but IT blocks agent access over security and governance.
- Nate hasn't used Azure in over a year, but sees this as **a governance problem, not a tech problem**. Talk to whoever owns IT (CTO, VP of engineering) to learn what rules and concerns are in place.
- Ask "what's the worst that could happen?" Examples: an employee changing data, data going to the wrong person, or data reaching Anthropic's servers (a separate issue).
- Take Azure out of the conversation. Focus on the data: where it lives and where it travels.
- Connecting non-technical staff to a live database is risky even with read-only access, because of what might go out to clients or around the company. Use **scoped keys and role-based access control**.
- **Follow-up: IT won't even allow read access. What now?** The operators already have access to one folder. The simplest start is a key that only works for that folder. Give agents the reach humans already have and nothing more.
- Sometimes the answer is to revisit it next quarter, when a rule is outside anyone's control.

### 2. Diogo: Is there a better cold outreach tool than SalesBlink? Should I send the emails manually?
- Context: Diogo is emailing local businesses from a new domain that SalesBlink is warming up.
- Budget matters. Cold email is a numbers game, and refining the offer and messaging takes time and money before you can scale.
- Nate doesn't know SalesBlink. If it works like Instantly (domain warm-up, campaigns, analytics), it's probably fine.
- **Add data enrichment.** Specific subject lines and opening lines with proof attached convert far better than generic copy. Nate uses Clay, which he thinks has the best B2B data.
- Don't send manually, or you become the bottleneck.
- Nate uses cold email and cold ads to **scale an offer that's already proven**. It isn't the cheapest way to start.
- Check every step of the funnel: where do people drop off, and why? Adjust the automation or copy, and document what works and what doesn't.
- **Follow-up: Is a 30-day warm-up, then 20–25 emails/day, normal?** Pretty typical (Nate says he's not a cold email expert). To start right away, buy pre-warmed domains, or buy several (say five) and warm them in parallel. Warm-up keeps you out of spam.

### 3. Melody: How do I test-market a discounted cohort training offer in my niche?
- Nate's "3 Ps": **pain, person, promise**.
- Context: Melody wants to train paralegals to handle bankruptcy work, which can become a turnkey service for bankruptcy attorneys. No specific training exists, and competitors have little visibility. She follows paralegal Facebook groups.
- The offer is brand new. AI told her it's viable, but "sometimes AI lies to me", and Nate agreed it tends to flatter.
- Test three things: the **title** (the benefit), the **promise** (what people can do once they finish) and the **price**.
- Free channels: Reddit communities and Facebook groups.
- With budget, **cold ads work better than cold email** here. Run ads for three versions of the offer and compare click-through rates. Meta or Google will push them to your avatar.
- Send clicks to a waitlist page with the title and promise. Don't call it "free", since it's a paid program.
- This is what Nate would do without his own community or email list.

### 4. Sam: What security tools and practices should a non-coder use when hosting company data in web apps?
- Context: Sam relies on the agents' own descriptions of the system and wants an outside sanity check.
- Use **other models and harnesses** to test the system's integrity.
- Search X or Google for well-known GitHub repos for security reviews and hack protection, then have your agents apply those practices. Nate does this with several harnesses on his own app.
- Then flip it: have a **swarm of agents attack your system** to find vulnerabilities. Use the most capable models (Nate named Fable 5.1).
- Models may refuse or "route down" because it looks like hacking. Show them the repo, prove the app is yours and explain it's for protection. If they still refuse, Opus 5 and "GPTSoul" also work.
- Standard practices: **rotate keys**, and store data so a breach in one part can't reach the whole system.
- These practices are old, so agents can add them easily. You just have to ask ("did you do this?").
- Add a line to `CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md` making security and safety the first priority. It changes how agents delegate and verify work.
- Learn from engineers with good thought leadership on this, such as Matt Pocock.

### 5. Jeffrey: What tools would you use to build an agentic marketing system for small businesses?
- Context: a deployable "marketing OS" that runs agents for clients with no marketing team, covering lead gen, intake, competitor research and social media campaigns.
- **What matters most is results.** Start with the one piece that's easiest to prove, usually lead gen (or social media).
- A lead gen system with proven monthly results is a stronger angle than an all-in-one OS.
- The best product answers one specific pain. Many small businesses only feel pain in one area at first.
- Add other modules later as add-ons, instead of trying to cut things out of a big package.
- Make onboarding seamless: the client enters their offer, proof and avatar in under an hour and starts getting results. The client still has to be able to convert those leads.
- Over time you end up with many mini agents in one client portal.
- **Follow-up: Which tools?** Claude Code plus Codex is Nate's go-to stack. He uses Fable for planning, brainstorming and specs/PRDs, then Astra to orchestrate agents for the build. Astra, Codex and Claude Code then critique each other's work and iterate. This could look different in 3 months.

### 6. Brandon: Where would you start restructuring an existing marketing agency to run leaner?
- Context: Brandon runs Google and Facebook lead gen with GoHighLevel as the CRM, and outsources account managers through Upwork. Too many steps (onboarding, marketing and client meetings) still need him.
- First, ask what clients value about working with you. Your moat is your IP and how you think, plus how clients feel. The services themselves are fairly standard. **Don't automate anything that hurts that.**
- Then map your job as a tree: branches are responsibilities, leaves are single tasks (e.g. "write YouTube description" and "write timestamps" under "make a YouTube video"). Manual, repetitive leaves are quick automation projects.
- Prioritize by your **constraint**: walk the business front to back and find where work piles up.
- **Follow-up: I'm supply-constrained and testing "Rockbot" as mini account managers. Is that the right idea?** Yes. More clients would mean hiring account managers and lower margins. Ask: "If 10 clients signed tomorrow, what would break first?" Automate that first clog (onboarding call, proposals), then ask again. Removing constraints is the only way to grow, and Nate asks his clients the same question.
- **Follow-up: Should I improve the current model rather than pivot?** Nate can't say without knowing more about the offer. Brandon noted that the done-for-you, full-service marketing expertise is the moat, so making it more efficient seems the right angle.

### 7. Action Taker: What should my non-technical business partner learn, and how should we split profit?
- Context: two beginners starting an AI automation agency. One handles the technical side, the other the business side.
- **There must be one final decision maker.** Equal ownership, responsibility and pay are fine, but someone has to break deadlocks. If you trust the business partner's judgment and you've been stuck for a week, he decides.
- Nate started solo and did development and project management himself, so he'd know who to hire, when, and how onboarding should work.
- A non-technical partner should get very good at **project management and hiring**.
- **Profit split:** define responsibilities first, then money and ownership. Doing it the other way round leads to uneven workloads and less commitment.
- **Follow-up: My partner doesn't know AI automation yet. Should I be the decision maker?** That makes sense. Discovery and sales calls are hard without understanding AI and the build, since the builder has to ask the right questions about data, decisions and who's involved. If you own more of the process from first touch to delivery, ownership and pay should reflect that.
- Keep the partnership flexible. Agree how you'll judge success and check that you're both pulling your weight about 3 months in.
- Action Taker also posted the question in the community for more perspectives.

### 8. Kinto: How do you build with Claude Code and Codex together and share context between them?
- Nate uses the Claude Code and Codex desktop apps, working inside his AIOS.
- Each one writes its progress to a **projects folder** in the AIOS. In the other app he says "look in the projects folder and see what Codex just put there."
- Both work from the same local data, so each always sees what the other did.
- Have them sign their deliverables ("delivered by Codex" or "delivered by Claude") so each agent knows who built what.

### 9. Mirc: Is it a bad idea to hire a salesperson who's new to AI for my one-person agency?
- Nate always used technical salespeople and technical PMs. Unlike info products or high-ticket sales, the salesperson also has to understand the requirements for the build.
- The test: **could the salesperson write the spec you'd hand an engineer?** With an AI novice, the answer is almost always no.
- **Follow-up: I only want them to find leads and pain points so I can run discovery. What then?** (A 90-minute call went in circles because the candidate kept asking what product to sell.) Hire an **appointment setter** instead. Define the role, responsibilities and job description, and judge them on performance.
- Appointment setters qualify leads, get calls booked and earn a bonus on every lead that converts, not just on booked calls. Mirc's plan of 10% once a deposit is paid and the contract signed fits that model.

### 10. Melody: Do Claude certifications give you more credibility with clients?
- A little, for now. It might help get your foot in the door, but it won't be the deciding factor. That's why Nate's team is building its own AIS certification.
- What wins is **proof specific to the client**: a framework you bring to the call, or a case study that matches their pain. The world's best personal assistant won't help if they want a sales agent.
- If it's not a big lift, get the Claude cert and use it as a secondary value prop. Don't lead with it.
- These certs are free or cheap and easy to get. Within a year everyone may have one, or they'll have no value.

## Key takeaways
1. **Corporate blockers are usually governance, not tech.** Find the worst case, follow where the data goes, and start with scoped keys that mirror human access.
2. **Cold email scales a proven offer.** Enrich leads with Clay, show specific pain and proof, automate sending, warm domains (~30 days, then 20–25/day) or buy pre-warmed ones, and track every drop-off.
3. **Test new offers on title, promise and price**, for free in Reddit and Facebook groups or with cold ads that point to a waitlist.
4. **Security for non-coders:** have other models and security repos review the app, then attack it with agents. Rotate keys, isolate data, and make security a priority in `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md`.
5. **Prove one module (usually lead gen) before building a full marketing OS.** Nate's stack: Claude Code + Codex sharing a projects folder, Fable for specs, Astra to orchestrate.
6. **Automate by constraint:** split your job into tasks, ask what breaks first if 10 clients signed tomorrow, and protect what clients value.
7. **Partnerships and hiring:** one final decision maker, responsibilities before money, and regular reviews. Hire appointment setters on conversion bonuses rather than AI-novice salespeople.
8. **Client-specific proof beats certifications.** Treat a Claude cert as a secondary value prop.
