### 1. Nolan: How do you split your time between Hermes and Claude Code?

### 2. Carlos: How would you use Claude Code and Hyper Agents in a business?
- For focused desk work, use Claude Code so you can run on the subscription.
- Hyper Agents gives $1,000 of free inference, then you pay API billing. It lets you switch models, runs in the cloud and has a more intuitive, button-driven UI.
- The $200/month Claude Code plan is worth about **$8,000** of usage if you max it out. Codex is worth about **$14,000**.
- AI is cheap right now and may not stay that way, so use subscriptions as much as you can. That's the main reason to pick Claude Code.
- Hyper Agents is a solid product (it sponsored the last event, and Nate vetted it first). It's just expensive because of model billing.

### 3. Alex: How do I set up one shared knowledge base for my dev team, with some things kept founder-only?
- Nate's team has worked on this for months and is building something more automated. Today's setup is a "band-aid".
- Google Drive is their data lake. One **team wiki** folder is readable by everyone, so anything pushed there is open to the whole team and their agents.
- Whoever pushes a doc owns it, and they and their agents can edit it.
- Leadership-only material is locked with normal Google Drive permissions by role or person.
- **The tech stack matters less than the people.** Slack, Notion, Drive or GitHub all work if people change habits: let agents read the shared source and keep pushing updates.
- Example: on GitHub, people work locally, forget to push and others pull stale data. Then the agents only measure human consistency, not progress.
- They're exploring crons on everyone's machines that auto-sync and check permissions, managed by an agent layer. Not proven yet.
- Every business owner they talk to has this pain point, so having an answer is valuable if you sell to businesses. Culture and habits first, then tech.

### 4. Christine: Would Codex work better than Claude for automating real estate video editing?
- Nate edits with Hyperframes plus FFmpeg to cut and assemble clips. Both tools work well, but he trusts Codex more.
- Re-test whenever a new model or harness update drops. His two YouTube videos where Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol each edited a full video from a similar /goal prompt give a quick comparison.
- **Verification loops matter most:** check that motion graphics land at the right timestamps from the transcript, and screenshot frames so nothing goes out of bounds.
- The first pass is rarely good with any model. Expect a 2nd, 3rd or even 15th iteration before it reports done.
- **Follow-up: How do you keep all the iterations organized and teach it your style?** Keep one project for videos. Each new video gets its own folder with version 1, 2, 3 saved locally, and you get the final render.
- Once you like a style, have the agent study the HTML code of your best few videos and turn that into skills and style guidelines.

### 5. Frank: Any tips for working with IP attorneys in a regulated industry?
- Frank's audio didn't come through, so Nate gave a general answer. There's no universal standard, and not every agency agrees with his approach.
- **Everything you deliver is the client's IP:** n8n workflows, prompts, skills, Python files, plus documentation on how it works and how to maintain it.
- The client's data stays theirs, and you can't use it.
- **Protect your know-how and reusable components.** Working with many clients, you'll spot patterns (such as gaps in lead gen), reuse code blocks or build a SaaS. Don't lock yourself out with IP clauses or non-competes.
- Clients mainly care about two things: they keep the code and automations if they stop working with you, and you won't use their data.
- Nate brought that position to the attorneys and asked them to protect it in the contract.
- They got pushback before the contract had an IP section. Raising it up front made conversations much smoother.

### 6. John: Have you tried building or fine-tuning an open-source LLM? Is it worth exploring?

### 7. Jeffrey: My AIOS has grown inefficient and keeps repeating the same mistakes. How do I fix it?
- One main AIOS with many projects and folders is fine. Nate's is huge too.
- **Treat CLAUDE.md as a router** at that scale: a table of contents pointing to where things live, not a list of behaviors.
- Put separate CLAUDE.md files inside the bigger project folders for project-specific details.
- Diagnose the mistakes: general conversation errors, or specific workflow errors? Build hooks or skills around the predictable ones to make the system as deterministic as possible.
- Segmented LLM wikis work well for Nate: one for YouTube transcripts, one for meetings, one for chats. The agent can drill down instead of searching everything.
- When it makes a mistake, show it the answer it gave, the answer it should have given and where that knowledge lives. Ask why it didn't find it, let it walk back the chain, then have it update its instructions.
- **Quarterly audit:** duplicate the project into a safe copy and have Fable (and Codex) review it against the Claude Code docs. It can suggest better routing, a flatter structure, and flag unused or bloated files.

### 8. Kenny: Can I trigger automations from a client's Instagram and Messenger inboxes with n8n?

### 9. Car: How much should a beginner charge to build SOPs for a client? Per SOP?
- Nate didn't give a price. He focused on scope instead.
- Automating a process that doesn't exist means doing two jobs (SOP and automation). It's harder to prove success, and the client will blame you if it fails.
- Don't tie your results to an SOP you invent, especially without expertise in that process. Help them formalize what's already in their head.
- **Start with one process** the owner or team already runs every day, and treat it like a discovery call.
- Get the people who run it on an hour-long call and screen-share a Google Doc or Sheet. Map when and why it starts, every data input, every decision and the context behind it, and who it touches (accounting, legal, etc.).
- Write bullets or a flowchart as they talk, and confirm after each step.
- **LRP framework: listen, repeat, poke.** Listen, repeat it back for confirmation, then poke with another question.
- That mapping is the first scope of work. If asked to build something you don't specialize in (say, their whole lead gen operation), say no. Under-promise and over-deliver.

### 10. Ronke: How do you build validation into an agent so it always pulls the correct data?
- **The answer is evals.** The design depends on the data source, inputs and outputs.
- If AI writes a SQL query, that needs its own eval. A simple primary-key lookup may not need AI at all.
- Build a **golden dataset**, such as 100 inputs with 100 human-approved answers. Run it and count how many come out right.
- For each miss, form a hypothesis and change one thing at a time. Example: 10 wrong because the SQL prompt was weak, fix it, rerun; 3 still wrong because the data was stale, fix the data.
- That's the only real way to prove a system with AI in it works.
- Non-AI lookups (a customer enters a ticket number) are close to 100% correct. Errors there come from typos or bad back-end data.
- List the error types your system can have and design the eval set around them.

### 13. Zeeshan: How do you use value-based pricing when a client won't share revenue or metrics?
- Some clients won't share salaries or revenue, and that's fair. Most of Nate's clients didn't push back because they saw his team as there to grow profit and cut costs and mistakes. Framing matters.
- Don't come across as collecting a case study. Offer an NDA if it helps.
- Frame it as: this is only a win for us if we save or make you as much as possible.
- **Pick one metric** for the first project. Example: support tickets resolved per week, from a baseline of 50 to 200 in two months. Then ask how valuable that would be.
- Value isn't only revenue. It could free two support agents to become managers or unblock a stalled project. These more emotional wins help show value.
- Ask "what would that do for the business?" rather than "how much more money will this make you?", which makes people defensive.
- Sell the destination, focus on one metric and position yourself as a long-term partner, not someone chasing a quick win.

## Key takeaways
1. **Pick the harness by context:** Hermes on the go, Claude Code or Codex at the desk, all on one GitHub repo. Codex is the pick for long, self-verifying tasks like video editing.
2. **Use subscriptions while AI is cheap:** the $200 Claude Code plan is worth about $8K of usage and Codex about $14K, versus API billing elsewhere.
3. **Shared team knowledge is a people problem first.** A readable team wiki plus permissions works only if everyone keeps pushing fresh data.
4. **Scale an AIOS** with CLAUDE.md as a router, segmented wikis, hooks or skills for repeat mistakes and a quarterly audit.
5. **Prove agents with evals:** a golden dataset, changing one thing at a time.
6. **IP:** the client owns deliverables and data. You protect your know-how and reusable components, and raise it in the contract up front.
7. **Selling:** scope one process as discovery, anchor value-based pricing to one metric and sell an AIOS as a consulting service.
8. **Tools:** ManyChat for Instagram and Facebook DMs, ElevenLabs for voice cloning and HeyGen for avatars.
