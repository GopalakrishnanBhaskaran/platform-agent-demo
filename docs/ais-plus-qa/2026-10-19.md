## Q&A

### 1. Hamza: Which business path gets to $100K MRR without chasing shiny objects?
- Any model works if you stay focused ("signal vs noise"). The real difference between models is how long they last.
- AI website agencies make good money now, but they likely can't charge those prices in about 2 years.
- Recommended path: consulting built on your product experience, plus a personal brand. Your moat should be expertise and relationships, not features.
- Start posting today on a steady schedule (LinkedIn, Medium or video). Nate hit $100K MRR with fewer than 100K YouTube subscribers.
- The math: 5 clients × $20K/month retainer = $100K MRR.
- **Follow-up: Should it include implementation?** Yes, because it supports higher rates. Win trust first with consulting hours, a paid workshop or a $1–5K project, then grow it into a retainer.
- **Follow-up: Which niche?** Pick one where your work ties to a business outcome people pay for (lead gen is a sticky example). Base it on your own experience and interest, not "top 3 niches" lists, which bring more distrust and burnout.
- Nate reached $100K/month as a generalist. His only filter was whether a client could afford $20K/month. Niche down later to scale, raise rates and run ads. After that, turn what you learn into a SaaS.

### 2. Mark: Can the AIOS be bundled with other software? How do I launch without a personal brand?
- Context: Mark's patented platform groups buyers into shared purchase orders, then suppliers bid in a reverse auction. Buyers' AI agents being online 24/7 would cut a 2–3 day wait down to hours. In effect it's an "RFP for everyday buyers".
- **Why will people let agents buy for them?** Convenience. With a trusted agent and spend limits, people will hand off more and more.
- **Bundling:** Expose an endpoint so agents can connect. Software agents can't reach will struggle over the next few years.
- **Launching without a brand, three options:**
  - Paid sponsorship: costs money up front and is short-term.
  - Co-founder: aligned with you, but takes a long time to find.
  - "Golden affiliate": **30–40% lifetime revenue share**, with no equity involved.
- Nate prefers one or more golden affiliates, or a co-founder. Find them in communities and through LinkedIn outreach. Smaller creators with potential tend to reply.

### 3. Enoch: Why Kylon? How do I set up an AIOS my team can share?
- **Kylon channels** let you:
  - Control which humans and agents are in each channel.
  - Add apps (such as dashboards) that the team edits, with an optional approval step for you.
  - Run scheduled automations 24/7.
- **"Brain agent":** One Kylon agent treats Notion as the source of truth and answers team questions from it. An automation twice a day writes new information back to Notion.
- **Can the team use my ChatGPT/Codex plan?** Yes. Connect the subscription as an agent in Kylon.
- **Skills built on my local Codex?** They're saved on your machine and only respond while it's on. Alternatives: hosted Codex (ChatGPT or Kylon cloud) or a ~$6/month VPS.
- **How does Kylon relate to the AIOS?** The AIOS lives in a GitHub repo, and Kylon agents work from that same repo.
- **Does the team need to learn GitHub?** No. You keep the repo updated, by automation or by hand, and they just talk to the Kylon agents.

### 4. Eric: How do I secure my AIOS? Is keeping it local enough?
- Everything is local by default. Securing the PC itself (Wi-Fi, malware) is a separate topic.
- Claude Code chats go to Anthropic. Never paste passwords, API keys or PII into a chat.
- Use a **private GitHub repo** and **turn on MFA** on every account.
- Keep secrets in `.env` and list sensitive folders (such as tax files) in `.gitignore`.
- Practical step: ask Claude Code to "help me lock everything down", review its research, then have it implement. Nate set up his VPS firewalls this way.
- There's no dedicated security module. The guidance is spread across the training and his YouTube videos.

### 5. Anon: What tools and steps do you use to make a YouTube video?
- **Recording:** OBS (free) for the screen plus a small 4K webcam. A good microphone matters most, and a ~$60 one is fine. Loom is a simpler option that gives you one file.
- **Editing and thumbnails:** Cut mistakes and dead space. Use Canva and/or GPT Image 2 for thumbnails.
- **Post-production:** Run the transcript through AI to get timestamps, description, a LinkedIn post and an X article. Nate built skills so the output sounds like him.
- **Follow-up (baking and fitness channels):** Entertainment content needs more lighting, editing and packaging. Look at Nate's Claude Code video-editing videos and repos.
- **"Roast room" for videos?** Yes. Create sub-agents or skills for about 3 audience personas and have them critique each video.

### 6. Matthew: Shared role skills and private memory per person behind company SSO. Hermes or a custom build?
- Each person gets their own AIOS for private memory.
- Shared skills go in one **team-skills GitHub repo**. You decide who can write to it.
- Everyone sets up a nightly automation that pulls new skills from the repo.
- Don't custom-build. Hermes is a free agent harness that gets improved daily and is good for always-on access from a phone. Claude Code or Codex also work.
- **Follow-up (50 non-technical corporate users):** The hard part is changing people's habits, not the tech. Pick the option with the least setup, which Nate thinks is Kylon. Matthew still needs to vet it with leadership as a vendor.

### 7. Ulysses: When is a VPS worth it? When should spreadsheets become a database?
- **Get a VPS** when your machine doesn't have enough compute (running models, rendering video) or something must stay **always on**. Otherwise you don't need one.
- **Move to a database** (such as Supabase) when you need role-based access, privacy, production automations that handle sensitive or user data, or team views limited to certain tables or rows.

### 8. Mike: How do I pay developers fairly without hurting margins, and how do I price my work as a developer?
- **Next live event:** June, in Salt Lake City. There may be a virtual one before then.
- **Payment options:** fixed contractor rate, full-time salary, hourly, revenue share or profit share.
- **Early on, Nate** paid developers only after the client paid, at **20–40% of project profit**. The risk: if a job is underscoped (say a $5K build), the developer may end up earning about "$2/hour".
- **Starting out:** Offer **20–30% of top-line revenue**. It's clearer than profit share and protects your margin. Hourly pay only works once you trust the logged hours.
- **At scale:** Salaries plus **5–20% profit share** per project, extended to PMs too. Only do this once deal flow is steady. "A smaller piece of a bigger pie."
- **As the developer:** Make sure you agree with the agency's pricing method, because if they underprice, you absorb the loss.
- **Working for an agency and running your own at the same time** is hard. It can create a conflict of interest and trust problems, especially under an exclusivity clause.

## Key takeaways
1. **Focus beats picking the perfect model.** Choose models that will last.
2. **Personal brand plus consulting and implementation** is the durable route to $100K MRR (5 × $20K retainers). Low-risk first offers build trust.
3. **Make products reachable by agents.** Agent-driven buying is coming.
4. **Team setup:** private AIOS per person, shared skills in a GitHub repo with nightly sync, and Kylon as the easy layer for non-technical users.
5. **Security basics:** no secrets in chats, private repos, MFA, and `.env`/`.gitignore`. Have Claude Code harden the rest.
6. **Infrastructure:** get a VPS only for compute or always-on needs. Use a database when access control or sensitive data is involved.
7. **Paying developers:** start with a revenue share, then move to salary plus profit share once deal flow is steady. Scope tightly to protect margin.
