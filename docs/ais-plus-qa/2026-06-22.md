# AIS+ Q&A: Jun 22, 2026

**Host:** Nate Herk · **Length:** 61 min · **Source:** Fireflies

## Q&A

### 1. Akai: I have almost no warm contacts. How do I start and grow a solo agency?
- **Context:** Akai has 15 years as a software developer, was a tech lead at an airline that shut down, and starts a new architect role (which AIS+ helped him land).
- **Your warm network is bigger than you think.** If someone would recognise your name, they're warm.
  - Go through your phone and email contacts and list them in a sheet.
  - Ask "do you know anyone who might be interested?" rather than selling to them.
- **Next, Upwork or Fiverr:** buyers have already posted a job, so they're warmed up. It's the second-best route to a first client.
  - It costs credits, and speed and bidding matter.
  - It frames you as an order-taker, not a growth partner, but it's good for first testimonials.
- **For a technical person,** the challenge is not overwhelming non-technical founders.
- **His wife's business is already a case study:** #1 in the Google Maps pack, 240% more website traffic, and 50% revenue growth.

### 2. Corey: Is n8n needed for workflows in Claude Code? Claude keeps steering me to it.
- **No.** Both can build the same automations. The difference is the interface and what you hand over.
- **n8n's advantage is one-click publishing.** In Claude Code you deploy one of these ways:
  - A routine (desktop app, or a schedule in VS Code)
  - More often, push the code to Modal or Trigger.dev
- Claude defaults to n8n because it sees the connected n8n MCP. Tell it you want it built in code.

### 3. Brad: I'm a solo consultant. When I'm busy delivering, my sales outreach stops, then I have to restart. How do I stay consistent?
- **Context:** Brad runs Strategy for AI in Dallas for professional-services firms. Projects grow into fractional Chief AI Officer retainers. He prefers building to selling.
- **Win more inside current relationships,** so you don't need sales sprints. Ideally that's three big clients you keep expanding.
- **Make your own business fully AI-native.** It scales you as a solo operator, and it becomes the framework you sell.
- **Builders rarely love selling.** A part-time or commission-only salesperson you call "off the bench" is a low-commitment fix once the pain keeps coming back.

### 4. Sahaj (follow-up): I'm switching to selling consulting hours, but automated LinkedIn outreach still books about one call a month. Any advice on copy or niches?
- **Volume:** 200 connection requests a week gets about 30–50 accepted DMs. Cold outreach takes about 10× what you expect, so aim for 300–400 messages a week.
- **Track every conversation.** Note where it stops (no reply? stalled after a few messages? "not now"?) and feed those reasons back into the copy. Watch the Savon cold-email video.
- **Use real case studies,** not exaggerated one-liners.
- **Pick the niche where you have experience or passion.** Cold outreach must remove risk and build trust fast, which is hard without knowing the industry's language.

### 5. Wasim: My pharmacy analytics app needs a continuous feed from BestRx, which has no public API. Other pharmacy systems (PioneerRx) can't even schedule CSV exports. What's a reliable, low-maintenance option?
- **When there's no API, use browser automation.**
  - Save a logged-in browser profile and script the export clicks.
  - Run it every few hours, and push the CSV to your app's back end.
- **It's the one approach that works across every pharmacy system.**
- Nate's favourite tool is the **Playwright CLI** with Claude Code. He pulls a weekly banking report this way.
- **Bot detection:** he's only hit it on heavily protected sites like LinkedIn. Don't poll every 20 seconds.

### 6. Mike Z.: What matters most when consulting for a business or department? I want my own repeatable method for AIOS engagements.
- **Meet them where they are.** Clients range from daily AI users to people who've never tried it.
- **Have a named framework,** as McKinsey (three horizons) and Bain (RAPID) do. It structures engagements and signals experience. AIS+ is building one for its certification.
- **Go after real constraints, not "vanity agents".** Tie each build to a KPI before building (say, refund rate from 10% to 7%).
- **The best systems run quietly in the background.** Systems that need a human in the loop grow linearly. Background systems compound: more leads, then better delivery and support, then more leads.
- **Proving value is the scarce skill now** that building is cheap. Most businesses use AI, but few report success, partly because no one knows how to evaluate it. (Some big companies' internal token leaderboards led to automations that burned tokens all night doing nothing.)
- **Also advise on unit economics:** subscription vs API billing, and routing tasks to cheaper models instead of sending everything to Opus.
- **For AIOS engagements, use the four Cs:** connect context and data first, then cadence (what runs on schedules or events). By the time you scope projects, you know the data and constraints. Expect multiple sessions.

### 7. Mike B.: I have 25–30 years selling ERP systems and doing business analysis. Can I sell AI audits and blueprints, and find developers to build?
- **Your differentiator is the experience,** not "what I've built". Lead with it.
- **Still build a few projects yourself.** Without knowing what AI can and can't do, it's hard to apply your expertise.
- **Partner with one developer to start,** build trust, and hand them detailed spec documents.
- **Automate your own processes first.** You'll find repeatable templates and skills. Then show a friend's business ("tell me honestly if this is valuable") before going to market.

### 8. Jeffrey: A large Chicago real-estate developer found my AIOS pitch intriguing. How do I run the discovery meeting?
- **Context:** his earlier pitches of specific real-estate tools got "cool, but we wouldn't use it". The developer already has an "AI guy" but liked the idea of a system that learns and improves with use.
- **An AIOS is a mindset shift more than a technical barrier,** so your job is to manage overwhelm.
- **Run the four Cs, plus grill-me:** connect context and data sources, then use grill-me so the client and their AI interview each other.
- **Overwhelm peaks when they first see files:** API keys, `.claude/`, `CLAUDE.md`. Explain the desktop app vs terminal vs VS Code, and take time there.
- **To win the engagement,** show your own AIOS and how it has evolved and helped you. The ROI is hard to communicate to someone who hasn't felt it.

### 9. Jiyaad: I'm starting YouTube for branding and client acquisition. What would you do if you started over? Does LinkedIn matter too?
- **YouTube plus LinkedIn is plenty to start.** Both are strong for B2B, and Facebook is also good for business. Repurpose every long video into a LinkedIn article with AI.
- **Know your avatar and your goal: views or conversions.**
  - Titles that answer a niche's pain point ("for plumbers") may get 10 views, with 4 booking calls.
  - Trend videos (new model releases) get thousands of views but few buyers.
  - The algorithm reaches whoever you package for. The subs-to-sales course covers this.
- **Pick a cadence you can keep for a year,** such as weekly. Growth is slow, then exponential.

### 10. Ready: How much should a beginner invest in learning n8n in 2026?
- **Very little.** Go straight to Claude Code or another coding agent. You can do far more, faster.
- The trade-off is deployment: Claude Code needs a separate host, whereas n8n has one-click publishing.

### 11. Andrea: A previous client wants a large automation for their luxury hotel. I can't do it all for free. How do I handle it?
- **Shrink the scope to a proof of concept.** It shows the automation is valuable and that you know what you're doing.
  - Do phase 1 of 5 free and hand it over: "it's yours, take it anywhere."
  - Then scope the remaining phases for a project fee or hourly consulting.

### 12. Lightning round
- **How do I deploy a product to a client?**
  - For SaaS, you manage infrastructure and scale.
  - For custom work: TypeScript on Trigger.dev, Python on Modal, routines on the client's own Claude account, or GitHub Actions.
  - See Nate's "3 ways to deploy Claude agents" video.
- **Matt: do you have a template deployment stack?**
  - Mostly Trigger.dev: a clean dashboard, an easy GitHub and Claude Code connection, environment variables and secrets handled, and cheap to run.
  - Python can go on Modal, or be converted to TypeScript.
  - Always hand over documentation: how to run it, where it lives, and which buttons to press.
- **Saddam: starting an AI automation vertical inside an established dev shop?** Productise the process you've already mastered (say, appointment setting) and become the best at it across industries. Build the brand and product around it.
- **Jinwoo: how complex can "vibe-coded" Claude Code automations get?** Don't vibe-code. Plan, verify and evaluate. See Nate's video with Cole Medin, "How to build effective AI agents in 2026".
- **Stephen: a client wants a years-old system rebuilt as self-sufficient and agentic?** Build an AI-first "digital twin" piece by piece.
  - Move one unit at a time, run it in parallel, retrain people on the new way, then retire the old version.
  - It's easier than transforming everything at once.
- **Mike: clients that are all-in on Microsoft — n8n with Claude, or Copilot?**
  - SharePoint and similar systems aren't automation-friendly. Nate's team had to "Frankenstein" custom solutions, with extra R&D.
  - Worth taking on, but research the systems before signing and price that work in. Under-promise and over-deliver.

## Key takeaways
1. **Your warm network is anyone who'd recognise your name.** Then Upwork, before any cold outreach.
2. **Grow inside existing clients,** and make your own business AI-native. It scales you and becomes your framework.
3. **Cold outreach is about 10× the volume you expect.** Track where conversations die and iterate on that stage.
4. **No API? Use browser automation** (Playwright CLI) with a saved profile, at a sensible frequency.
5. **Consult with a named framework,** tie every build to a KPI, and favour quiet background systems that compound.
6. **Sell an AIOS by managing overwhelm:** the four Cs, grill-me, and your own system as the demo.
7. **Shrink big asks into a free or cheap proof of concept,** then scope the remaining phases.
8. **Deploy custom work to Trigger.dev or Modal, with handover docs.** Price unfamiliar systems (like Microsoft) for R&D.
